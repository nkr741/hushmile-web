Hushmile privacy policy

Last updated 24 October 2026. Applies to the Hushmile Android app (app.hushmile.journey) and the web viewer at nkr741.github.io/hushmile-web. This is the privacy policy linked from Google Play.

In short

Hushmile lets someone share one road trip with the people they choose. A family journey needs no account. From app version 1.4.4 a travel company can create a desk account. Since app version 1.3.0 Hushmile runs its own small server (api.hushmile.com) that relays encrypted updates and passes place search and traffic times to Google. It cannot read journey updates. Those updates are encrypted on the traveller's phone with a key made from the Journey Code and (from app 1.4.1) a random link key that travels only in the shared link. Only someone with that code can read them. We cannot read them. We do not sell personal data.

What the app uses

  • Precise location, only after the driver starts a journey and while it runs (shown by an ongoing notification). It stops when the journey ends or the code expires (18 hours).
  • The name the driver types, the start and destination they choose, vehicle type, planned route, battery level and whether it is charging, stops, kilometres covered and left, and check-in answers (All fine, Break, Traffic, Need help / SOS).
  • Only if the traveller adds them (app 1.4.2+): in passenger mode, the cab's vehicle number, driver's name and cab app or company; and the traveller's own phone number saved in Settings for SOS, which is put in the encrypted update only while an SOS is on, so the family can call from the alert. Both are encrypted like everything else and are never part of the Journey Code.
  • The family phone sends only "seen" and "please check in" signals, also encrypted.

We do not use advertising, analytics, crash reporting, contacts or photos, and no phone number except the optional SOS number described above. On Android the app registers with Google Firebase Cloud Messaging when it starts. This gives the app a random installation ID and a push token that Google uses only to deliver wake-ups. They are not linked to your name or Journey Code, and uninstalling the app removes them from the phone.

Travel desk

Drivers and families still do not sign in. A travel company that wants a booking list does. Creating that account sends us the company name, an email address and a password. We store the password only as a PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash with a random salt and 210,000 iterations. We never keep the password itself. The sign-in token is stored only as a SHA-256 hash and expires after 30 days.

A booking stores the vehicle number, the from and to text the company typed, who the trip is for (a cab, a school bus, a truck or an employee cab), an optional watcher name, the time they should start, and the status (not started, on trip, or arrived) with those times. It does not store the Journey Code or any location. The company gets two different links. The driver link can start that one booking after the driver enters a 4-digit number, which is not inside the link. The server keeps only a hash of that number and a hash of the start secret. The watch link can only follow the journey. It cannot start it, end it, or open the company's other bookings. After the driver taps Start, the link they were sent can no longer end the trip. The phone that started it can.

Security

  • The app allows only HTTPS. Journey updates use AES-256-GCM. The server carries the sealed message and cannot open it.
  • The Journey Code is not a field on the travel-desk API. A request that includes one is rejected.
  • Company passwords and session tokens are stored only as one-way hashes. Sign-up and sign-in are rate limited.
  • Deleting a company account requires the password again, so a saved session alone cannot wipe it.
  • We have not commissioned an independent security audit. These are the controls the software actually uses.

Who can see a journey

Anyone the driver gives the Journey Code to. They see the exact position on a map, its latitude and longitude, the nearest place name, battery level and whether it is charging, stops, time and distance left, and the last update time. Share the code only with people you trust.

Services the app talks to

  • Hushmile relay (api.hushmile.com, a self-hosted ntfy server in Bangalore, India) carries the encrypted updates. It keeps each sealed message for up to 19 hours and cannot read it. It sees the time and the phone's internet (IP) address, which it uses in memory for rate limits and does not write to its logs in normal operation.
  • ntfy.sh (public message relay) is the fallback when the Hushmile relay does not answer. It keeps each sealed message for about 12 hours and cannot read it. It sees the time and the phone's internet (IP) address.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging wakes the family phone when there is news. The wake-up carries only a random channel name and the words "New message": no place and nothing from the update. Google sees that the phone follows that channel and when it was woken.
  • Google Maps Platform (Places, Routes and the street name of where the phone is), reached only through the Hushmile server, for destination search, the address of this phone when a journey is started, and traffic-aware travel times. Google receives the text you type and the phone's position to name the street and rank results, the start and destination of the drive, and, while a journey runs, the phone's current position about every 15 minutes to update the time left. These requests come from the server's address, without your name, your phone's internet address or the Journey Code.
  • OpenStreetMap services (Nominatim and Photon for place search and place names, OSRM for driving routes and time, and OpenStreetMap map tiles). These receive the place you type or a position, over HTTPS, without your name or code, under their own privacy policies.
  • Weak-signal stretches (app 1.3.1+). The family's app (when it opens a journey) or this viewer (when the phone goes quiet) asks the Hushmile server which spots along the route often lose signal, so a gap there can read as expected. It asks for whole areas of about 40 x 20 km, never the route or the Journey Code. Only if the driver turns on Help map weak signal in Settings (off by default) does the end of a journey send one anonymous report: a list of squares of about 1 km where updates could not get out for 3 minutes or more, with the hour of the week and a rounded length. It has no code, name, exact times or order, leaves out everything within 2 km of the start and the end, and goes from the phone's internet address, which is used only for a daily limit. The server keeps only a count and usual gap per square, not the report, and shows a square only after 5 different journeys reported it.
  • Link key (app 1.4.1+). Each journey has a random link key that travels only in the shared link (after the #) and is never sent to our server in readable form. A journey opened only through the link stores nothing about the key on the server. Only when someone follows with just the 8-character Journey Code does the traveller's phone store the link key on the Hushmile server, locked with a key made from the Journey Code by scrypt (a deliberately slow, memory-hard function) with a random salt for that journey. The server keeps only that locked copy, the salt, a lookup id made from the code the same slow way, and a check value for deleting it; it never holds a usable key. The locked copy is deleted when the journey ends and expires within 19 hours in any case. To be honest about the limit: a code-only journey is only as strong as the 8-character code, the slow lock, and the server's lookup limit for each internet address. Sharing the full link is stronger than reading out the code.
  • Nearest town, stops and rain (app 1.4.1+). While a journey runs, the app asks the Hushmile server for the nearest town (about every 5 km), what a stop of 5 minutes or more is at (fuel, food or a toll plaza), and whether it is raining heavily (about every 30 minutes). The server asks OpenStreetMap (Overpass, Nominatim) and Open-Meteo, and Google only if those do not answer, from its own address, with the position and nothing else. Answers are kept by area (about 1 to 11 km) so one answer serves many journeys.
  • Hospitals and police on the route (app 1.4.2+). When SOS is used, or someone taps Hospitals and police nearby, the app asks the Hushmile server for the nearest ones; the server asks OpenStreetMap (Overpass) from its own address with the position only, and keeps the answer by area (about 5 km) for a week. The web viewer instead offers a Google Maps search link near the last position, opened only if you tap it.
  • If the driver chooses to text someone or call for help, the phone's own SMS or dialler app is used. If the driver chooses to open the destination in Google Maps, the phone's Google Maps app is used.

This website and web viewer

The pages the app and Play open are the static pages at nkr741.github.io/hushmile-web, hosted on GitHub Pages. The name hushmile.com is not that site yet, so it is not the privacy policy. The website uses no cookies, analytics, trackers or third-party fonts. The Journey Code stays in the link after the #, which browsers do not send to the web server. The page decrypts updates in your browser and only connects to the Hushmile relay and ntfy.sh, and loads OpenStreetMap map tiles for the area of the route (OpenStreetMap sees your internet address and this site's name, never the code or the route itself). GitHub may log basic request data (such as IP address) under its own policy.

Keeping and deleting data

Journey details on the phone are kept only for the current journey and the last arrival screen. Ending a journey sends a "closed" notice so the code stops opening it, and relay copies expire after about 19 hours (12 hours on ntfy.sh). Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage deletes everything on that phone, including a saved company sign-in.

A family journey is not linked to a name or email on our server. What the server keeps for it is encrypted messages that expire, a locked link key that expires, and anonymous weak-signal counts that are not tied to a person. A company account is linked to an email. Its bookings last 30 days, or until the company deletes the account in Travel desk. To ask us to delete anything else, email the address under Contact. We reply within 30 days, confirm what we hold, and delete what we find that is linked to you.

Children

Hushmile is meant for adults aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Who operates this

The app and the server at api.hushmile.com are operated for Hushmile from India. The relay runs in Bangalore. Questions, access requests and deletion requests go to the contact below. This notice describes the data the software actually handles. It is the notice for the app, the web viewer and the travel desk.

Not an emergency service

Hushmile does not contact emergency services and never calls anyone by itself. SOS (hold the button for 3 seconds, then a 5 second countdown you can cancel) alerts the people following the journey through the same encrypted updates; it only works when the phone has signal, and nobody may see it in time. The app offers 112 in the phone's dialler; you press call. In an emergency call 112 (India) or your local number.
